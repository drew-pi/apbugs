### background

Hey, my name is Andrew (as you probably already know). I'm originally from Brooklyn, NY but now I go to Cornell University some 4 hours north of NYC in Ithaca.

I study computer science and math (double major). I discovered my love of programming completely by accident. During COVID, my parents pushed me to do some online Python classes during which I built my first rudimentary game. After that I started experimenting - making half baked passion projects like a crypto trader inspired by my friend who made $2,000 from Ethereum (after losing 10 dollars I quit this specific project). Still, working on these projects in my free time made me realize how much I enjoyed building things. I was drawn to the process of breaking a project into smaller problems, solving each one, and using it as the foundation for the next. In high school, I joined a computer science specialization that taught me many of the core fundamentals I needed to grow as a programmer. That experience helped me secure my first internship after my senior year. When I got to college I began taking more theoretical classes and really developed a love-hate relationship with mathematical proofs. Even though sometimes it was extremely frustrating, I feel like the process of constructing a rigorous argument step by step is so satisfying and really beautiful so I ultimately picked up math as a double major.

I'm also a big sucker for philosophy especially modern philosophy such as existentialism but also post metaphysics (like Michel Foucault) and feminism. Although I haven't had much time to take courses on these topics in college - I still look for opportunities to learn more.

Outside of the technical, I'm generally a really outdoorsy person so I love skiing, hiking, swimming and anything that involves being active. It's even better when an activity combines more than one of these things like hiking with skis or hiking to a swimming spot. In middle school and high school I ran track but now at Cornell I run recreationally in the brutal hills of Ithaca. I also love reading and recently I got a Kindle which has opened a world of opportunity for me. I also love museums and the opera but not ballet. I also speak a few languages - I am obviously fluent in English but I also speak Russian fluently and am pretty solid at French although I need more practice :)

### career goals

My goal is to become a software engineer at a company with a strong developer culture, where I can learn how experienced teams design, build, and maintain high-quality software. I see software engineering as both a science and an art: building systems at scale requires technical rigor, but collaborating with hundreds or thousands of engineers also depends on shared principles, clear communication, and thoughtful development practices.

I am particularly interested in environments where I can learn how to create reliable, maintainable software at scale. I also enjoy exploring new tools and programming paradigms. At the moment, I am focused on strengthening my low-level programming skills, particularly my understanding of memory management, system performance, and optimization.
